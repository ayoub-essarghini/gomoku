# Gomoku (Five in a Row) - Complete Rulebook

Welcome to the comprehensive guide for Gomoku, also known as Five in a Row. This document outlines the core rules of the game as well as the advanced starting conditions used to balance competitive play.

## 1. The Core Rules

Gomoku is an abstract strategy board game played with Go pieces (black and white stones).

- **The Board:** Traditionally played on a grid of **19x19** intersections.
- **The Setup:** The board begins completely empty. One player takes Black, the other White.
- **Turn Order:** Black always plays first. Players alternate turns, placing one stone per turn.
- **Placement:** Stones are placed on the empty intersections of the grid. Once placed, a stone cannot be moved or removed.
- **Winning Condition:** The first player to form an **unbroken line of exactly five stones** (horizontally, vertically, or diagonally) wins the game.
- **Overlines:** In standard casual play, a line of 6 or more stones (an "overline") also wins. However, in strictly balanced rules (like Renju), overlines do not win for Black and are considered a foul.

## 2. The First-Player Advantage & Starting Conditions

Because the board is open and Black moves first, Black has a mathematically proven, overwhelming advantage. To mitigate this and make the game fair, competitive Gomoku utilizes various **Starting Conditions** (Opening Rules). 

Here is the evolution of how the game is balanced:

### Standard (No Restrictions)
- **Mechanic:** Black plays the first stone in the center (Tengen). Players alternate freely.
- **Fairness:** Completely broken. Black wins 100% of the time with optimal play. Used only in casual play.

### Pro Rule
- **Mechanic:** 
  1. Black places their 1st stone exactly in the center.
  2. White places their 1st stone anywhere.
  3. **Restriction:** Black's 2nd stone (the 3rd move of the game) must be placed outside a central 5x5 grid.
- **Fairness:** Slightly delays Black's victory, but ultimately still unbalanced.

### Swap Rule (The "Pie Rule")
- **Mechanic:** 
  1. Player 1 places 3 stones on the board anywhere (2 Black, 1 White).
  2. Player 2 evaluates the board and chooses to either play as **White** or **Swap** colors and take Black.
- **Fairness:** Much fairer. Player 1 is incentivized to make a balanced opening, otherwise Player 2 will just steal the advantageous color.

### Swap2 Rule (Current World Championship Standard)
- **Mechanic:** 
  1. Player 1 places 3 stones anywhere (2 Black, 1 White).
  2. Player 2 has three choices:
     - **Stay White:** Play the 4th stone.
     - **Swap Colors:** Take Black, handing White to Player 1.
     - **Add 2 Stones:** Place 1 Black and 1 White stone anywhere on the board, and **pass the final color choice back to Player 1**.
- **Fairness:** Perfectly balanced. The threat of adding two stones and passing the choice back prevents Player 1 from setting up tricky, disguised advantages.

## 3. Renju (Alternative Balancing)

Instead of swapping colors like Swap/Swap2, the **Renju** variant balances the game by applying strict fouls **only to Black**:
- Black cannot create "Three-and-Three" (two simultaneous open-ended rows of three).
- Black cannot create "Four-and-Four" (two simultaneous rows of four).
- Black cannot win with an overline (6+ stones).
White is immune to these restrictions and can use them to force Black into a foul, winning the game.
